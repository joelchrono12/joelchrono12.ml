A couple of weeks ago I acquired a tiny ereading device, the XTEINK X4. It all started with a YouTube video [reviewing the device](https://youtu.be/uGpAAKEHjlo) that I first shared in [one of my weeknotes](/blog/2026-w10/#youtube). A day later I saw that [James had been using one already](https://bne.social/@james/116204477104692183), and soon enough after a few more reviews I [ordered one myself](/blog/e-ink-is-very-cool/).

Along James, some other people I follow on the fedi got it, such as Neil, [who shared his thoughts on it too](https://neilzone.co.uk/2026/03/initial-thoughts-on-the-tiny-xteink-x4-ereader/). 

And because of me (yes, I'm taking all the credit ;) [Jeremy got one](https://polymaths.social/@jp/statuses/01KKKWEZ53P9E078P4839PS9YW), [wwwgem got one](https://www-gem.codeberg.page/sys_x4/), [Robert got one](https://gts.da-miez.de/@irgndsondepp/statuses/01KNC8B6V0R1Q2MV3Y9SA7FZGM), [njrk got one](https://im-in.space/@njrk/116221119639099884)... so on and so forth. A quick look through [the hashtag](https://mastodon.social/tags/xteink) will let you know that its popularity has been plenty for a few months now.

There really are not a lot of reasons not to give it a go. With a price of 70 bucks or much less on a good sale, there is no excuse, in my opinion.

The XTEINK X4 is a mini ereader with excellent battery life, a great set of physical buttons and, best of all, a community building homebrew for it and making it better day after day!

The most popular firmware for it, [CrossPoint](https://github.com/crosspoint-reader/crosspoint-reader) has managed to optimize quite a lot of things compared to the built-in OS. In fact, I didn't even use the device until I got it installed, which took me less than ten minutes.

All I did was change the language, plug the device to my dad's Windows laptop---because I couldn't bother to figure out permissions for the serial USB access on my Linux-powered laptop, sorry not sorry---and off I went.

I went for a minimal and more manual approach. I only have eight books on it, and I will only be replacing them as I go through them. The device doesn't have a lot of memory, with less than a megabyte of RAM, so it's ideal to [format the epubs accordingly](https://github.com/bigbag/epub-to-xtc-converter) and the like. [@www-gem](https://www-gem.codeberg.page/) has done a lot of work finding links and extra tools, so definitely check out his posts on the device.

I already finished one book on it, and I am making progress on another two. The reading experience is excellent and I really have no complaints about it other than the lack of a backlight. I carry it almost everywhere, and reading one or two pages---often many more---at any point is painless, as it wakes up in just a couple seconds from sleep.

The reading experience so far has been fantastic and the portability is really awesome. I don't know what else to say, I've not even charged it since I bought it!
