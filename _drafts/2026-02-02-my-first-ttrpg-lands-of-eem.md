For the past few months, my friend Dave has been working on his own TTRPG, documenting his misadventures on his [personal blog](https://lategamer.bearblog.dev/its-not-procrastination-its-a-side-quest).

Time and time again, a game mentioned as inspiration has been [Land of Eem](https://landofeem.com/), a whimsical and family friendly take on the genre that seemed like a perfect mix for my friend group.

So, after bothering Dave on Signal, asking questions and advice, I ended up acquiring the [core rulebook](https://landofeem.itch.io/land-of-eem-core-rulebook) as well as the [free Quickstart guide](https://landofeem.com/), which included the initial adventure that we all played together.

## Some backstory

For a long time I was not familiar with what a TTRPG even was, I had heard about *Dungeons & Dragons* here and there. I still have vivid memories of watching *Ned's Declassified School Survival Guide* featuring a sort of stereotypical nerds dressing up as wizards and playing with miniatures, making it all seem rather cringe to kid me. I don't actually remember much of this, maybe TTRPG fans were happy and making memes about themselves. 

Anyway, with later shows like *Stranger Things* bringing it up to a mainstream audience, I started to think more about how cool it would be to live our own adventures and the like.

Even later, with my own discovery of the *Dungeons & Daddies* actual play podcastz and even my favourite gaming podcast  *Into The Aether* doing a short series playing a DnD campaign titled *Out of the Realm*, interesting grew even stronger.

Point is, I knew that I would eventually fall on to this, but there was one hiccup in the way.

This is gonna be a bit cringe for some atheist readers or people who don't care about religious stuff. Feel free to skip to the [next section](#preparing-for-the-first-session).

Of course I'm talking about the stigma against *Dungeons & Dragons.*

Look, I know what some will say, "you shouldn't care about what others think", "it's just a game, there's no real demons and whatever", "those who think that are dumb and you shouldn't interact with them."

But we are talking about my parents here, and I choose them above whatever an online comment says. They have grown a lot since my kid days, they used to be the kind of people who were worried about *Pokémon* and *Harry Potter* being demonic. And the same was true for D&D.

Things are better now, and they respect my decisions, but even then, as a believer myself, I still felt a bit weird about a game where people can call upon X or Y deity or demon, and whatever. It's not because I think it's real (after all, I play videogames like *Hades*), but I also don't live in my own house, and I don't set my own rules, *and* I don't get to make some of the younger friends in my group play a game their parents may not approve of.

Besides, D&D seemed much more complicated and full of depth that I didn't quite want to get into, especially because I would be the one directing the whole thing.

So, when I saw how *Land of Eem*  was a less dark, less obscure, more family friendly, more focused on characters and relationships and avoiding conflict, more wholesome overall, and much simpler to get started with, it was the obvious choice.

## Preparing for the first session

After buying the game, I watched a variety of videos talking about it. Reviews, critiques and the like. Everyone kept praising pretty much every aspect of it.

I shared the news to my friend group, and everyone was really excited about it, some of them created their own characters in advance, others decided to use some of the premade ones available with the Quickstart assets.

Since pretty much everyone is a complete noob for this stuff, I was doomed to become the Game Master, put in charge of the world, NPCs and keeping things fun for everyone.
