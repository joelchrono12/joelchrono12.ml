I usually take the bus to work, this is provided for free by my workplace.

Today however, I was on a rush, I got out of my house way too late and I knew I would have to run if I wanted to make it in time...

I didn't want to run.

Instead, I knew that I could make it to the opposite side of the neighborhood and catch a regular bus. If it arrived on time, I could rendezvous with my work transport just in time.

The public bus failed me.

It got to the stop with only one minute left on the clock. I could still catch up to the work transport if the driver was a little late, which happens sometimes.

My hopes paid off, I saw the bus waiting for me and I was only a couple streets away in a direction where the driver would see me running towards the vehicle.

I got off the public bus and ran, my hands up to let the driver know I wanted to get in. Still waving my hands and walking towards the bus, it started moving towards me, I made it---I was only a dozen steps away from it now.

Then it drove past me.

Well, swinging my arms around as if I was stranded on an island and a plane flew by. I ran after the bus making myself as visible as I could, I even managed to catch up with it for a few seconds, before it inevitable sped up. It didn't see me on the mirror, or at all. Or maybe the driver chose to ignore me because I was late and the guy was grumpy this morning.

I was left there stranded with no clear idea of what to do next.

I send a message to my mom though she doesn't see it, I think of calling her, I think something else instead---a risky bet that could pay off.

My work bus takes a pretty roundabout route to pick a bunch of people up, the public transport is more streamlined, and there is one spot where both transports meet again, which means I could still take the route to work from there---if everything goes well.

Another public bus is taken, and I think about some other options. Scrolling for a bit, I consider complaining in the the bus chat group. There, I remember a coworker who recently got a car, so I call and ask for a ride. Thankfully, he agrees to help me out, though there's a catch---the public bus has no stops near his place.

There are two options: hope the public bus catches up to the work bus and hope the work bus sees me this time, or walking for about ten or fifteen minutes and have a sure way to get to work.

I can depend on variables I don't control, or I can submit myself to walking what I should have walked from the start.

I learned the lesson now, don't you worry.

We settle on a spot about ten minutes away, I have plenty of adrenaline rushing through me by now because of the stress of the whole thing I don't even feel tired after the fact.

As I make it to the pick-up spot, something hilarious happens. I see the work bus on its way! I am too far to get on it this time, but now I know I could have met up with it if I had followed my original plan. Something to consider next time.

In any case, my friend asks me if I can make it to another spot instead, only about three minutes of walking distance, totally fine at this point.

A friend from church drives by, sees me and asks me if I need a ride or anything. Truly divine providence and pretty unexpected stuff. I'm so thankful, but I let him know I already have someone to pick me up and he goes his way.

My mom finally messages me worried, telling me I should have called her, she says she's at the gym and it's late and all of that. I let her know all is fine and send her a pic of where I'm at. I'm waiting for my friend in that very same gym.

Soon after my ride arrives and we head to work like nothing happened.
