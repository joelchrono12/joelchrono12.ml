Today I decided to talk about the 88x31 buttons that can be found online in plenty of old school websites, I have already [mentioned them before](/blog/free-time-warhammer-site-buttons/#adding-web-buttons), and [I've also done a few of them](/blog/webrings-and-the-small-web/), because they are simply very cool!

Recently, I've been adding more buttons my [blogroll](/blogroll), such as [mercurial's](https://writeblankspace.netlify.app) (what a cool gif!), [Syl's](https://syls.blog) (loved the background), [Marisabel](https://marisabel.nl) (super retro and cute!) and [James'](https://jamesg.blog) (I don't know why I didn't for so long).

I have also made my own buttons for some projects from friends or clubs I'm on. I did one one for the [Clew search engine](https://clew.se) and the [Cookie Zero Club](https://edleeman.co.uk/cookie-zero), which you can see in [the footer](#cool-buttons) section of every page.

![Clew](/assets/img/badges/clew.webp)
![Cookie Zero](/assets/img/badges/cookiezero.webp)

The rest of the buttons I've made were for bloggers! I created one for [Burgeon Lab](https://www.burgeonlab.com/), which was a nice challenge because of the logo, and one for [Anhvn](https://anhvn.com), which I feel doesn't represent her site well at all, even though it's her favicon, but well it'll do.

![Burgeon Lab](/assets/img/badges/burgeonlab.webp)
![Anhvn](/assets/img/badges/anhvn.png)

In any case, trying to represent something in a limited space such as the 88x31 button is a very interesting excercise in creativity, as is the case with all of pixel art.

If you have a website and you don't have a button yet, I encourage you to try and do one yourself! And I will link to it if you share it with me.

I do all of my pixel art using [GIMP](https://gimp.org) but I also found this [online site](https://hekate2.github.io/buttonmaker/) to make your own button with some pretty simple configuration options available.
